# Concept and origin

Percent Grading began with Yubin Kim's question about valid fragments inside an incorrect answer. The A/Z example is an idealized decomposition metaphor, not a claim about every font or a working visual recognition algorithm.

Evaluate the submitted answer under a stated task. Separate components, relationships, conditions, and goal fulfillment. Do not infer a person's complete knowledge or ability from one response.

A rubric percentage describes weighted fulfillment, not the probability that an answer is correct. A model's confidence is separate. Missing required information can justify zero; unresolved judgment requires a reason for abstention.

Different assumptions can support different conclusions. Preserve the assumptions rather than averaging incompatible meanings into an apparent truth percentage.

The framework extends the engineering use of partial credit, analytic rubrics, and evidence-based evaluation. Its distinctive choices and practical benefits must be tested, not assumed.
