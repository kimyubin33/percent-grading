The specified apple appears red under broad white illumination. Its skin reflects relatively more long-wavelength visible light and absorbs relatively more of the shorter wavelengths. The light reaching an ordinary human observer therefore produces a red appearance. Apparent color depends on illumination, surface reflectance, and the observer; changing those conditions is a different scenario. A future trial could apply an opaque blue coating to a separate matched apple. If the coating reflects predominantly blue wavelengths under the same white illumination, that coated specimen could appear blue. Compare the reflected spectra and color observations of coated and uncoated specimens under the same light and viewing conditions.
